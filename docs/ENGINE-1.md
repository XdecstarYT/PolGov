# Engine 1 — Democracy & Political Engine

Coverage against the 150-feature brief, written to be useful rather than
flattering. Three categories:

- **Built** — a real system with mechanical consequences and tests.
- **Light** — present and working, but thin: usually a field, a label, or a
  single action rather than a system.
- **Not built** — absent. Listed individually at the bottom with what each
  would take.

Roughly **103 built, 28 light, 19 not built**.

---

## What the engine actually does now

Five systems carry most of the weight. They are worth understanding as
systems, because the feature list undersells how much they interact.

### The electorate (101–120)

Twenty overlapping voter segments, each with its own position, turnout habit,
weighting of eight issues, and volatility. A region is no longer a single
ideology vector — it is a *mix of people*, and its politics are emergent from
that mix.

The chain runs: state of the country → a score on each issue → weighted by
what a segment cares about → its verdict on the government → its vote, shaped
by ideological proximity. Every link derives from state already simulated.
Nothing says "retirees like health spending"; it falls out of their issue
weights meeting the actual state of the health service.

This is the foundation everything else stands on. Elections, referendums,
policy popularity and campaign channels all read from it.

### Electoral systems (19–30)

Five ways of counting the same votes: first past the post, proportional,
mixed-member, preferential with transfers, and two-round runoff. They
genuinely disagree — measured disproportionality runs from ~1 (proportional)
through ~10 (mixed-member) to ~16 (FPTP) on the same electorate.

Districts are generated by splitting a region's electorate, conserving every
segment exactly. That conservation is what makes **boundary redrawing**
meaningful rather than magical: a gerrymander cannot create a voter, only
decide which district they wake up in. Both classic moves are implemented
literally — packing hostile voters into a conceded seat, cracking your own
support out of a safe one into the marginals. A full redraw is worth roughly
17 seats, which is why the practice is contentious.

### The party you lead (31–50)

Four factions, generated relative to whatever platform the player chose, each
holding a share of the party's MPs. A wing that finds a bill too far from
where it stands **withholds its seats**, and those come out of the seat
arithmetic before the chamber is counted — so it is entirely possible to hold
a comfortable majority and lose a division to your own side.

Parties also have their own money, separate from the national treasury.
Campaigning is paid for out of it. (A governing party billing the state for
its election advertising would be a scandal, not a strategy.)

### Two chambers (51–75)

The Senate is renewed by **halves**, so half of it was elected by a previous
electorate, and it uses proportional representation whatever the lower house
uses. A government that has just won a landslide still faces a chamber
reflecting the last one. Money bills bypass it, because an upper house that
can block supply can starve a government it cannot remove.

Around that: committees that cost a month and return a bill better drafted and
less distinctive, amendments that buy votes and water bills down ~18% each
time, crossbench deals, closure motions, and question time whose outcome
depends on the record the player actually has.

### The life of a law (76–100) and the campaign (121–150)

Policy popularity is *computed*, not authored — a bill's appeal to each
segment falls out of what it does to the issues they weight. Laws take one to
two months to be felt, can carry sunset clauses, and can be repealed (which
unwinds standing arrangements but not money already spent). Manifesto promises
are judged at the end of each term, with breaking one costing more than
keeping one pays.

Six campaign channels reach different segments — television lands with
retirees and never reaches students; door knocking is the most persuasive
thing available and costs volunteers rather than money. Polls are *samples*:
the player never sees the true figure, small polls can be several points out,
and two polls the same week disagree.

## Light

Working, but thin. Each is a field, a label or a single action rather than a
system, and each would repay being deepened.

| Feature | What exists | What is missing |
|---|---|---|
| Regional elections (3) | Regions are electoral units | No separate regional government to win |
| PM selection (6) | Largest party forms a government | No separate investiture vote |
| Snap / early elections (10, 11) | One action, 40 PC | No timing strategy beyond the cost |
| Election scheduling (12) | Fixed 12-turn terms | No variable-length parliaments |
| Campaign HQ / staff (14, 47, 48) | One investment level | Staff are folded into the HQ number |
| Party registration (17) | Party setup screen | No registration process or thresholds |
| Rename / logo / colours (32–34) | All present | Cosmetic only, as intended |
| Opposition / government leader (56, 57) | Largest parties identified | No named leaders with traits |
| Cabinet (59) | Posts allocated to partners | No portfolios or named ministers |
| Committee hearings (62) | Committees report | No hearing as a playable scene |
| Parliamentary debates (64) | Debates exist in campaigns | No chamber debate outside question time |
| Confidence votes (69) | Collapse path exists | No explicit confidence motion the player can call |
| Filibusters (71) | Risk computed, closure available | Risk is not yet applied at the division |
| Parliamentary negotiations (73) | Crossbench and coalition covered | No general vote-by-vote bargaining |
| Regulatory change / programmes / projects (94, 96, 97) | Covered by the bill catalogue | No distinct mechanics of their own |
| Interest groups (118) | Segments have preferences | No organised lobbies acting on their own |
| Media interviews (135) | Press conferences | No separate set-piece interview |
| News cycles / breaking news (139, 140) | News feed generated per turn | No salience cycle changing which issues matter |
| Recounts (148) | Close seats identified and reported | The recount is not re-run |

## Not built

Nineteen features, with what each would actually take.

**Multi-tier government (2, 4, 5)** — state, local and presidential elections.
The largest gap. Each tier needs its own body, its own election cycle, and a
mechanical reason to care about controlling it (regional delivery modifiers
are the obvious hook). This is a tranche of work in itself, not a feature.

**By-elections (9)** — a seat falls vacant mid-term and is re-run alone.
Straightforward on the existing district model: the machinery is all there.

**Candidates (15, 16, 18)** — registration, eligibility, and independents.
Needs a candidate object per district with quality and incumbency, which would
also make district contests more textured than pure segment arithmetic.

**Party constitution (35)** — rules governing leadership challenges and
candidate selection that the player could amend. Factions cover some of the
same ground.

**Speaker, shadow cabinet, ministers (55, 58, 60)** — named individuals with
traits and competence. Would give reshuffles real content; currently a cabinet
post is a number.

**Private members' bills (65)** — bills originating from backbenchers,
including hostile ones. A natural fit with the faction system: a rebellious
wing tabling its own bill is a good pressure mechanic.

**Constitutional amendments (80)** — entrenched provisions needing a
supermajority plus a referendum. Referendums exist; the entrenchment does not.

**Emergency legislation (81)** — fast-track powers during a crisis, with a
civil-liberties cost. Would pair naturally with the existing event system.

**Policy reviews (92)** — a formal look-back at an enacted law, with the
option to modify it. Distinct from repeal.

**Public consultations, petitions, lobbying (98, 99, 100)** — three routes for
outside pressure to reach the desk. `PC_COSTS_POLICY.consultation` is defined
but not yet wired to anything.

**Fact-checking (138)** — claims made in debates and press conferences being
checked afterwards, so overclaiming backfires. Needs claims to be objects
rather than prose.

## Where the balance stands

Measured, not assumed. A 42-game stress run across all three difficulties:
**41 active / 1 collapsed over ~3,900 simulated turns, zero crashes.** Bills
carry at roughly 75%. Each of the five electoral systems completes a full
multi-term game.

Four balance problems were found by measuring rather than reasoning, and all
four are documented in the commit history: invisible debt, a leadership
challenge that removed 42 leaders out of 42, districts that were statistical
clones of each other, and seat projection bands that collapsed to a single
number.
